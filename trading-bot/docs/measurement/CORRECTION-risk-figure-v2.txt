THE POSITION TOOL'S "Amount" FIELD — DECODED
  Amount is the ACCOUNT BALANCE at that leg, on a $1,000 nominal account.
  It is not the money at risk and it is not a P&L.
      risk   = 1000 - Amount(stop leg)
      reward = Amount(target leg) - 1000
  So "Amount: 750" on every stop leg means $250 risk, every time, and the
  reason it never changes is that it is a template setting, not a live figure.

  The $1,000 base is DERIVED, not assumed. Solving (amtT - B)/(B - amtS) = R:R
  for B on three frames from three different years returns exactly 1000.00
  each time. Nothing else reproduces the printed ratios.

WHAT I GOT WRONG, PRECISELY
  v1 said I misread the digits. I did not. I read them correctly and modelled
  them wrong. Tested against my own three TBOT1 trades:
      trade              qty*stop   1000-amtS   qty*target   amtT-1000
      TBOT1 4:11  BTC      249.50      250.00       790.61      792.20
      TBOT1 22:59 HYPE     250.00      250.00      1249.04     1249.04
      TBOT1 1:09  OM       250.00      250.00       741.75      741.70
  The target labels I dismissed as misreads — "1792.2", "2249.04", "1741.7" —
  decode to rewards of 792.20, 1249.04 and 741.70, matching quantity x target
  distance to within 0.2%, and the HYPE one to the penny.

  So v1's advice to "treat the printed Amount as decoration" was exactly
  backwards. The field is highly reliable. I just did not know what it meant.

  One label WAS genuinely misread: the BTC stop percentage. The printed % is
  entry-relative, confirmed on the other two trades (HYPE 0.779/21.287 = 3.660%
  vs printed 3.66%; OM 0.38142/5.18166 = 7.361% vs printed 7.363%). So BTC
  should read 2.04%, and my "1.84%" was a real misreading. The arithmetic
  separates the true misreads from the mis-modelled ones.

MY PROPOSED CROSS-CHECK WAS PARTLY CIRCULAR — CONCEDED
  I suggested verifying that risk x R:R = quantity x target distance. Since
  TradingView computes the printed R:R as target distance / stop distance:
      risk x R:R = (qty x stop) x (target/stop) = qty x target
  It is an algebraic identity and holds for any quantity, including a wrong one.
  It is still worth running as a digit-reading check — a misread digit in any of
  the four fields breaks it — but it cannot tell you what the Amount field
  means, because it never touches that field. Calling it "two independent
  routes" was wrong; it was one route plus a self-consistency test. The Amount
  decode is the genuine second route, and it agrees.

Q6 — WHERE IT NOW STANDS
  Settled: the DRAWING convention. Risk is $250 on a $1,000 nominal account,
  unchanged across eight trades spanning 2022-11 to 2025-06 (five public frames
  plus three from the TBOT1 recordings). Codeable as R = 250, base = 1000, with
  targets readable straight off the Amount fields.

  Not settled, and probably not settleable from frames: what he actually risks.
  $250 on $1,000 is 25% per trade, which is a teaching device, not a live sizing
  rule. No frame anywhere shows a real account size or a real percentage. Treat
  the $250 as a unit of illustration — useful for reading his intent off any
  chart, useless as a bot parameter.

  Consequence for the bot: derive position size from your own risk budget and
  his stop distance. Do not import 250, and do not import 25%.

      S8 1:03:38  DOGE 2h   entry at fraction 1.00 of the box (bottom edge)
  Most likely reconciliation: "the 50% line" is the equilibrium of the impulse
  LEG, which is standard supply-and-demand language and a different object from
  the midpoint of the order-block BOX. Both statements can be true at once if
  the box happens to sit at the leg's midpoint.
  This must be resolved before either rule goes into software. They imply
  different entry prices on the same chart.
