PART 1 — CURRENT TRUTH. THE RULES THAT SURVIVED.
================================================================

ZONE CONSTRUCTION                                    confidence: high
  Zone boxes span candle BODIES, not wicks. Held on every frame, all passes.
  Some zones are drawn as two stacked bands: inner darker BODY band, outer
  paler WICK band reaching the wick extremes. Where both exist, entry geometry
  uses the BODY band only; the wick band is used only to locate the stop.
  Worked example, BNB 1D 2023-10-24:
      wick band   242.20 to 248.56   (pale)
      body band   237.79 to 242.20   (darker, bordered)
      entry       237.74             (body band bottom edge, 0.5 px)

ENTRY PLACEMENT                                      confidence: high
  Entry sits on the PROXIMAL edge of the body band, the edge price reaches
  first. Long into demand from above goes to the TOP edge. Short into supply
  from below goes to the BOTTOM edge.
  Five confirmations, two directions, four instruments, 2023 to 2025, two
  independent measurement methods:
      2023-10-24  BNB 1D    SHORT   bottom edge, within 0.5 px
      2024-06-09  BTC 1D    LONG    top edge, within 0.5 px
      S8 1:02:28  DOGE 2h   SHORT   fraction 1.00
      S8 1:03:38  DOGE 2h   SHORT   fraction 1.00
      S8 1:28:33  SOL 4h    LONG    fraction 0.01
  ONE MEASURED EXCEPTION, real and not an artefact:
      TBOT1 1:09:19 OM 1h   LONG    fraction 0.173, a sixth inside the zone
      Re-measured with getImageData, R:R gate passed (2.971 vs printed 2.97),
      and a full-width row scan found no nested band that would put it on an
      edge. Rows 544 and 608 only, nothing at the entry row 555.
  PENDING TEST: does prior penetration of the zone explain it? Hypothesis is
  edge entry on a virgin zone, inside entry where price has already swept
  through. Untested at time of writing.

STOP PLACEMENT                                       confidence: medium
  The stop is placed AT a structural level, not at a computed distance. The
  level is whichever is nearest beyond the zone: the far edge of the zone's
  wick band, the far edge of a separate deeper zone, or a plain drawn
  horizontal level.
  Measured, with sign showing whether the stop clears that level:
      2023-10-24  BNB 1D   +15.5 px   +1.78    0.71% of price   beyond it
      2024-06-09  BTC 1D   -14.5 px   -526.4   0.83%            INSIDE the band
      2022-11-30  BTC 4h    -1.0 px   -5.60    0.03%            on the line
  The signs do not agree, so "just past the edge" is wrong. What holds is
  "placed at the level", with hand-placement slop up to about 0.8% of price on
  either side. Snap the bot's stop to the level and model no offset.
  Qualitative constraint that held everywhere: the stop is below the candle
  bodies and above the deepest capitulation wick. It deliberately does not
  clear the capitulation wick.

TARGETS                                              confidence: low
  No fixed rule. Observed reward-to-risk across eight trades:
      1.60, 2.06, 2.13, 2.27, 2.61, 2.97, 3.17, 5.00
      median 2.44, mean 2.73, six of eight between 2.0 and 3.2
  If a default is needed, 2.5 sits at the centre of his behaviour. That is a
  description, not a rule he follows.

POSITION SIZING                                      NOT DERIVABLE
  His charts show $250 risk on a $1,000 nominal account, unchanged across eight
  trades from 2022-11 to 2025-06. That is 25% per trade, so it is a teaching
  template. No frame anywhere shows a real account size or real percentage.
  Derive size from Rhys's own risk budget and the stop distance.

PART 2 — WRONG. DO NOT REVIVE THESE.
================================================================
Each of these looked solid and was published in an earlier draft. They are
listed rather than deleted so a fresh session does not rediscover them from the
same faulty reasoning.

  "Stop sits roughly 0.5x the zone height below the zone bottom."
      From three samples reading 0.479, 0.487, 0.582. It is an artefact of which
      band gets nominated as "the zone". The same frames yield 0.042 to 4.145
      depending on that choice, a factor of 99. Three clustered samples were the
      same arbitrary choice made three times, not a rule.

  "Risk per trade is a fixed $750."
      Misreading of the position tool's Amount field. See the decode in Part 5.
      Real figure is $250, and it is a template regardless.

  "The printed Amount labels were misread digits."
      They were not. The digits were correct and the model was wrong. The target
      labels dismissed as misreads decode to rewards matching quantity times
      target distance to within 0.2%.

  "The stop clears its structure by a fixed gap."
      Tested as pixels, percent of price, percent of stop distance, and fraction
      of zone height. Inconsistent on all four, sign not even stable.

  "Entry is at the 50% line of the zone."
      A caption cue suggested it. The 50% line is neither entry nor DCA. It sits
      near the body band midpoint, about 5.7% out on a 38.5 px box, and has no
      established role. Off the critical path.

  "Stop sits 79 units below the lower band on 2024-06-09."
      Superseded. That reading took a stop label box's bottom edge for the stop
      line. Corrected, the stop is 526 units INSIDE that band.

PART 5 — METHOD. HOW TO READ ANY FUTURE FRAME.
================================================================

THE POSITION TOOL DECODE
  Amount is the ACCOUNT BALANCE at that leg on a $1,000 nominal account. It is
  not risk and not P&L.
      risk   = 1000 - Amount(stop leg)
      reward = Amount(target leg) - 1000
  Printed % is entry-relative. Printed R:R is target distance / stop distance.
  The $1,000 base is DERIVED, not assumed: solving (amtT - B)/(B - amtS) = R:R
  for B on frames from three different years returns exactly 1000.00 each time.

THE VALIDATION GATE, use on every frame
      (entry_row - target_row) / (stop_row - entry_row)  ==  printed R:R
  A frame that fails is unreadable, not an estimate. This gate caught a stop
  label box's bottom edge being read as the stop line, and correctly refused two
  frames whose semi-transparent fills could not be resolved closer than ~10 px.

CALIBRATION
  Derive price-per-pixel from the position tool's stop leg, which prints its own
  price span. Do NOT calibrate from axis labels: TradingView pushes overlapping
  labels apart and their rendered positions are not their true prices.

TRAPS THAT COST REAL TIME
  - Never combine a measurement taken in one magnified view with a price read in
    another. That single mistake produced both wrong answers in this project.
  - Position tool label boxes carry white halos sitting on box edges. Read edges
    on columns well clear of any label.
  - In a live browser player: seeking while paused often fails to repaint and
    returns a stale frame. Use seekTo(target-3), play, poll currentTime past the
    target, then pause.
  - Playback quality silently collapses to 144p while still reporting "hd720".
    Force hd1080 before and after each seek and verify videoWidth is 1920.
  - Browser caption harvesting is DEAD, not flaky. The caption element latches
    one real cue per page load and binds it to every timestamp queried, and
    which cue it latches varies per run. Measured: 21 captures returned one
    identical string; a fresh reload returned different text for the same
    timestamp; capture rate 5%. It produces genuine sentences attached to wrong
    timestamps, which reads as a clean transcript and is confidently wrong.
    Use yt-dlp locally instead.
  - The channel's Videos tab under-renders in the DOM. Read window.ytInitialData
    and follow continuations via /youtubei/v1/browse. Tiles are lockupViewModel,
    not videoRenderer.
